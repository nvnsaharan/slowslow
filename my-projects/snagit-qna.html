Product and fleet
Q1What is SnagIt in one sentence?

A backend plus three Android apps that run scraping jobs on a fleet of consumer phones over residential IPs, and deliver the results as files to a B2B customer.

Q2Why phones and not servers?

Targets block data-centre IPs and bot-like clients. Real phones on residential networks look like normal users.

Know the catchThe fleet is not owned, so battery, data and consent become design constraints.
Q3Who are the two external parties and why do they never talk?

The customer producer (publishes batches to SQS) and the phones (poll HTTP). We sit between so each side runs at its own pace.

Q4How do you get phones?

Users install our comparison apps (Comparoo, Compario, Comparion) and the app contributes background scraping in exchange for the tool and points.

Know the catchConsent must be explicit. Compario defaults FleetConsent to no.
Q5What is a "walk"?

A job that collects data for one store or category, up to about 45 minutes, with heartbeats. Short jobs like search are about 30 seconds.

Q6What job types exist?

Blinkit assortment, product_listing, category discovery, US ride routes, and per-store catalogue walks. Lease lengths differ per type.

Q7What is the difference between the three apps?

Comparoo: India grocery plus rides (com.automation.jediapp). Compario: US rides (com.jedi.us). Comparion: Africa rides, Tanzania default, negotiated fares never badged cheapest.

Know the catchAfrica has no backend cell yet.
Q8How do users earn?

The Earn feature runs background scraping and awards points. The apps removed earn/points messaging on the marketing site for Compario.

Architecture and service split
Q9Why four services from one image?

Split by resource profile and scaling signal. dispatch scales on request rate, artifactworker on build queue depth, orchestrator and control are fixed. One image keeps one toolchain and one build.

Say thisThe split is by what the process does to the machine, not by domain.
Q10Why does dispatch have no database handle?

So its scale can never multiply DB connections. v1 scaled on queue depth and exhausted the pool. The doc checks that go list shows no pgx in cmd/dispatch.

Q11Why Go instead of staying on Python?

Measured 47,345 req/s with p99 3.4 ms in Go vs 5,325 req/s and 17.2 ms in FastAPI. The decision was also about maintainability and one binary. Parity tests found four defects.

Know the catchBenchmarks are laptop numbers.
Q12What are the orchestrator loops?

intake, settler, sweep (30 s), reap (10 s), outbox (5 s), shelf (5 s), reconcile (60 s), revocations (60 s), retention (hourly), backlog (60 s), fleet (60 s).

Q13How do multiple orchestrator replicas avoid doing the same work?

No leader election. SQS visibility timeouts, a Redis consumer group, and FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED partition the work.

Know the catchAdmission control still needs a fixed count.
Q14What is the control plane here?

control service, app_config, revocations, admission control and reconcilers. They decide rules; dispatch carries traffic.

Q15How do you evolve APIs when installed apps cannot be updated?

A compatibility shim serves v1 paths in v2, and a minimum version code gate stops old builds that cannot behave.

Know the catchTemporary token bridge cannot verify tokens.
Q16Why a producer API for US routes?

Customers could not publish to SQS directly for rides. POST /v2/producer/us-routes accepts routes with expiry and an hourly rate limit and queues a batch.

Know the catchDefault minimum expiry is 30 minutes because ride intake shares the queue with Blinkit bursts.
Redis, Lua and queueing
Q17Explain the shelf.

Per type and priority sorted sets holding job ids, a hash of payloads, a zset of leases and a hash of lease metadata. Dispatch pops the first non-empty key in the device plan order.

Q18How do you guarantee one device per job?

claim.lua uses ZPOPMIN, which removes the member as it reads it, inside single-threaded Redis. No race and no row to vacuum.

Say thisAtomicity of the script is the guarantee.
Q19Why encode priority in the key?

First non-empty key wins is correct global ordering only because the caller sorts keys highest first. It also means a miss is a few O(1) calls on mostly missing keys.

Q20Why not a Postgres queue with SKIP LOCKED?

At 1,667 claims/s delete-on-claim creates about 144M dead tuples a day. Eligibility was the argument for SQL; encoding it at enqueue removed it.

Know the catchreclassify.lua moves already queued work when eligibility changes.
Q21What does the reaper do?

reap.lua takes expired leases (bounded per call), restores the original score so the job keeps its place, requeues, and buries after max attempts into a dead set.

Know the catchDead set lives only in Redis.
Q22Why restore the original score?

A reclaimed job should not go to the back. The trade-off: a failing job holds the head of its lane until max attempts.

Q23What was the complete.lua bug?

The holder was read as the last field of lease metadata. After claimed_ms was appended, it compared device id to a timestamp, every release returned 0 and no lease was torn down. Fixed by matching trailing digits explicitly.

Say thisAppending a field to a packed string broke a positional parse. Now matched explicitly and tested.
Q24Why do late results return 200?

A late but genuine result is a success. v1 returned 400, the phone counted it as its own failure, and three of those ended a run.

Q25What is the drain rate and why count it in Lua?

Jobs leaving the shelf per minute. Counted with one INCR on the hit path (about 17% of calls) so admission control uses the real fleet speed rather than an assumption.

Q26What is a Redis hash tag and why use it?

Braces like {in} in the key force keys into the same slot so multi-key Lua stays legal on cluster. The cost is a cell pinned to one slot (about 100k claims/s).

Q27What happened with float scores?

now_ms + position/1e6 was rounded away in float64, making FIFO arbitrary silently. Fixed with integer ticks.

Postgres, ledger, outbox and idempotency
Q28Why keep Postgres if Redis serves jobs?

Redis is derived and can be lost. Postgres is the ledger that can rebuild the shelf and prove what was settled.

Q29What does "ledger first, shelf second" prevent?

A crash between them. Reverse order leaves a dispatchable job with no ledger row that cannot settle. Correct order leaves a ready row not on the shelf, and the reconciler re-fills it.

Q30Why settle before releasing the lease?

If Redis is lost after release, both result and lease are gone while the ledger says outstanding. Settling first makes the worst case a redo, never silent loss.

Q31How are retries idempotent?

job_id = blake2b(batch_id||msg_id), seen_messages guards intake, and the settler status-transition claim makes a second settlement a no-op.

Q32Explain the outbox.

Messages owed are rows with sent_at NULL. The drain claims with SKIP LOCKED, sends, marks sent. Locks stay held across the send to avoid deadlock with a split design.

Know the catchAt-least-once, so receivers must dedupe.
Q33Why partition completions by month?

Retention can drop an old partition cheaply instead of deleting rows. ensure_completion_partitions creates future ones.

Q34How do you protect Postgres connections?

Budget 0 + 32 + 32 + 4 = 68 against role limit 80. A test recomputes it from config.json.

Q35Why batch 500 results per transaction?

0.018 transactions per result. At design load 73.1 txns/s instead of 1,667/s.

Failure modes and reliability
Q36Phone dies mid-job.

Lease expires, reaper requeues with the original score, next phone picks it up. After 3 attempts the job is buried.

Q37Redis is lost.

Pending results and leases die together. The ledger says outstanding, reconcile rebuilds the shelf, a phone redoes it. Wasteful, never silently wrong.

Know the catchNo automatic failover or backups today.
Q38Postgres is down.

dispatch is unaffected (no DB). Settlement stalls and results queue on the Redis stream. Orchestrator retries.

Q39S3 is down.

dispatch returns 503 with jittered Retry-After. The phone retries; the ResultOutbox keeps unsent results.

Q40Why 503 and never 500?

The client treats non-404/429 as its own failure and stops after three. 503 plus Retry-After means come back later.

Q41What is the silent-loss risk?

Work older than SQS retention is hard-deleted with no error or ledger row. blinkit-in retains 5 hours, design assumed 14 days. backlog loop reports headroom_s.

Say thisI would name this before they ask.
Know the catchThe fix needs another team to change retention.
Q42A poison message?

DLQ with redrive. Throttle retry must outlast the redrive window (fix on 5 Oct).

Q43Fleet drains slower than supply.

Admission control caps intake to drain_rate x deadline. Supply 1,667/s vs drain about 9.3/s is a 180x gap.

Q44What caused the 5,000 rejected submits a day in v1?

Empty walks were rejected, each counted as a phone failure, stopping 753 devices (about 12% of fleet). v2 returns 200 on an empty walk.

Q45How do you detect trouble?

16-stage log vocabulary to Loki, three Grafana dashboards, 18 alerts. A fleet-drain-collapse alert had never been able to evaluate until it was fixed.

Security and auth
Q46How do phones authenticate?

Device token (HMAC-SHA256, 30-day max, epoch) from POST /v2/enrol, and a work token bound to job and device with 1 h TTL.

Q47Why is the work token TTL longer than the lease?

So a slow but genuine completion after lease expiry is still provable and can be accepted.

Q48How does revocation work?

Revoking bumps the device epoch. The orchestrator republishes revoked devices to Redis every 60 s. The epoch was once never compared; now it is.

Q49Is guest enrolment safe?

It mirrors v1: any presented string enrols. It is a known weakness; real verification is on the list.

Know the catchThe v1 token bridge also cannot verify tokens.
Q50What secrets exist and where?

Secrets Manager entries injected with :field:: JSON-key suffix. Redis has a 40-character password, plain redis://, shared security group.

Know the catchNo TLS to Redis. Task role has AmazonSQSFullAccess.
Q51Leased credentials?

The orchestrator leases a provider account credential for a job, and logs redact it. A test proves a leased credential actually authenticates a walk.

Q52Any legal risk?

Bolt adapter uses a private rider API and the README warns to check terms. Fleet consent follows Play policy.

Clients and Android
Q53Why gomobile?

Shared Go provider logic with TLS fingerprinting and parsing, exposed as one caller package in an AAR. UI stays native Kotlin/Compose.

Know the catchProviders drifted between India and US copies.
Q54How does background scraping run?

WorkManager worker with a shared ScrapeCycle mutex. An Android 15 crash from a restricted foreground-service type led to running inside the worker.

Q55What happens offline?

ResultOutbox stores unsent results (55 min, 50 entries, 1 MB). The server sets next poll. 404 retries after 3 s and 8 s.

Q56How do pushes start work?

FCM silent_sync messages start a cycle. Message types: normal, cta, feedback, silent_sync. In the US the orchestrator sends them every 30 min inside the work window.

Q57How did you cut Comparoo latency?

Three causes: EncryptedSharedPreferences rebuilt on every construction (60-90 ms each), Moshi falling back to reflection (about 480 ms, three times) because codegen was missing, and sequential store calls. Connect tab went from 3-4 s to 20-340 ms.

Say thisMeasure first: I could name three concrete causes.
Q58What is DemoMode?

For Play reviewers. The server ships only the SHA-256 of the access code and demo mode borrows a sample pickup when location is denied.

Q59ProviderConfigSync?

Backend can override provider hosts, headers and field names through /rides/provider-config, applied only when version increases. Lets us fix a provider change without a release.

Q60How are ride fares ranked?

Cheapest, Fastest, Best value. Negotiated-fare providers are never badged. It never books, only deeplinks.

Know the catchValue-of-time numbers in Africa are assumptions.
Q61Local storage and its risks?

Auth token in EncryptedSharedPreferences (plaintext fallback if MasterKey fails). Provider sessions in unencrypted bbolt. Cart is in memory only.

Q62Testing?

About 20 unit test files in Comparoo, 125/127 pass. No Android CI workflow yet. Backend CI runs real Redis and Postgres.

Deploy, observability and cost
Q63Why the arm64 pin?

Cluster is all Graviton t4g.xlarge. An amd64 image dies with exec format error before any log reaches Loki, which looks like silence.

Q64Why a separate target group and /v2/health?

v1 checks /health which v2 does not serve. Registering v2 in the v1 group fails every check and ECS kills tasks in a loop that looks like an app bug.

Q65How was cutover done?

Added v2 by rules first, tested, then moved the ALB default action. First attempt on 25 Sep was rolled back; v2 serves the fleet since 28 Sep. v1 is at desiredCount 0.

Know the catchRollback is no longer one call.
Q66How do configs stay correct?

config.json is the single source rendered into task definitions. Tests fail if a variable without a safe default is unset.

Say thisMissing REDIS_URL would dial localhost and look like an empty shelf.
Q67What does it cost?

About $30/month idle. At design load S3 PUTs would be about $43k/month, which is why presigned uploads matter.

Know the catchNo budget alarm.
Q68How is retention handled?

retention.sql with bounded purge functions and monthly partitions. S3 lifecycle: staging 1 day, artifacts to IA at 7 days, Glacier IR at 30.

Scale and capacity math
Q69How many polls per second and how many jobs?

Design budget: 10,000 polls/s and 1,667 jobs/s. About 83% of polls are misses.

Know the catchBudgets, not an end-to-end run.
Q70Why is fleet drain the real limit?

10,000 devices at about 9.3 jobs/s total means 10,000 batches would take about 15 hours. Hence admission control.

Q71What breaks first as you scale 10x?

A single Redis slot per cell (about 100k claims/s), the single Redis host, and Postgres being shared. Split cells by routing and use a dedicated Postgres.

Q72What is the connection budget?

68 of 80: dispatch 0, orchestrator 32, artifactworker 32, control 4.

Q73Footprint at 500k queued jobs?

Redis 477 MB, Postgres 331 MB (measured).
